P000 [Normal] CHAPTER IV
P001 [Normal] DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
P002 [Normal] This chapter presents the findings for FurniCraft: An AI-Based Furniture Recommendation System for Lance Furniture Shop. The discussion is arranged according to the three objectives of the study: (1) identifying the existing furniture recommendation process of the establishment, (2) describing the features of the developed system, and (3) testing its usability. The account of the existing process and the description of the system functions are based on the information documented in Chapters I to III. The usability findings are based on the aggregated System Usability Scale (SUS) tallies from 52 respondents supplied by the researchers.
P003 [Section] The Existing Furniture Recommendation Process of Lance Furniture Shop
P005 [Normal (Web)] The customer workflow begins when a customer opens FurniCraft and checks whether they already have an account. New customers create an account and verify their email, while existing customers enter their login credentials. If the credentials are invalid or the account is not verified and active, the customer must correct the details, verify the email, or contact the shop. After signing in, the customer browses furniture and opens Plan your room.
P006 [Normal (Web)] The customer saves furniture preferences, then chooses whether to use a room photo. If they use one, they upload or select a saved photo and decide whether to allow AI analysis; otherwise, they continue without a photo. The system ranks furniture and displays a 2D preview when available. Customers can refine their preferences until satisfied, then select an item and submit a reservation request. The request is saved as pending for shop review; the customer can track its status after the shop approves or declines it. The diagram notes that a 2D preview is an estimate and a reservation does not guarantee stock, measurements, or payment arrangements.
P010 [Normal (Web)] The admin workflow starts with signing in and checking that the login details are valid, the account is verified and active, and the account has admin access. If any check fails, the administrator follows the diagram’s recovery path, such as correcting login details or signing in with an authorized account. Once admitted, the administrator opens the dashboard and chooses a task: manage the furniture catalog, reservations, customer accounts, reports, privacy or API information, or room placement. YES selects a task; NO continues through the task choices. If no task is selected, the administrator returns to the dashboard.
P011 [Normal (Web)] For reservation management, the administrator reviews the customer and furniture details, visit or customization notes, and any available preview. The administrator can approve, decline with a reason, cancel an open request, or leave it unchanged. The system saves the decision and status history and queues notifications for applicable updates. For an approved request, the administrator can later record item readiness or close it as completed, claimed, or no-show. Other admin tasks include adding or editing catalog products, activating or deactivating customer accounts, exporting reports as CSV or PDF, reviewing privacy and API information, and assessing room placement. After completing a task, the administrator can return to the dashboard for another task or log out. Approval does not guarantee stock, payment, or item readiness.
P018 [Normal (Web)] The flowchart shows Lance Furniture Shop’s sales process before the system was introduced. It begins when a customer asks about or orders furniture. An admin or staff member discusses the customer’s needs, budget, and style, then sales personnel present available products or wood and varnish samples. The shop then determines whether the customer wants a ready-made item or a customized order.
P019 [Normal (Web)] For ready-made furniture, staff check inventory at the branch or contact the warehouse, quote the price, agree on a delivery or release date, and complete a paper reservation receipt while collecting cash or a down payment. They prepare the item for pickup or dispatch. If pickup or delivery is unsuccessful, staff handle the issue, such as a return or delay. For customized furniture, the admin discusses dimensions, wood, and finish; manually prepares specifications and cost estimates; fills out a paper job order; records the down payment; and schedules production with the workshop or artisans. If the order is delayed past the promised date, staff follow up by call or SMS.
P020 [Normal (Web)] The shop records completed transactions manually in a sales record book. When the customer claims the order, staff collect any final payment, issue a paper receipt, and release the furniture. If an order is never claimed, the shop records it as cancelled or unclaimed, which may result in wasted materials and storage space. The process ends with sales and issues handled through manual paperwork, phone calls, and staff coordination.
P022 [Normal (Web)] The flowchart shows the customer’s manual furniture-selection process at Lance Furniture Shop. A customer visits a physical branch, examines and compares display items, and asks sales personnel for recommendations. The customer then tries to judge whether the furniture’s design, size, wood, and finish will suit their needs and space. If a suitable ready-made item is found, the customer agrees on its price and delivery or release date, and the shop provides the product on the promised date.
P023 [Normal (Web)] If no suitable ready-made item is found, the customer decides whether to order customized furniture. If not, the customer leaves the store. If yes, they specify the design, dimensions, wood type, and varnish, place the order, and agree on a release date. The flowchart then checks whether the customer claims the order on schedule: claiming it leads to a successful purchase, while an unclaimed or cancelled order may result in wasted materials and financial loss. For ready-made furniture, a failed fit or unmet expectations may lead to dissatisfaction, extra expense, or a return.
P026 [Normal] Lance Furniture Shop provides ready-made and customized furniture, including wooden doors, bed frames, dining sets, cabinets, tables, and chairs. As described in the background of the study, customers consider several product attributes during selection, such as design, measurements, material, finish, and price. The establishment offers wood selections such as Gmelina and Mahogany, as well as varnish finishes including Natural, Narra Look, Mahogany, and Chocolate Brown. The conventional selection process depends on the available furniture information and assistance provided by shop personnel.

P085 [Section] Usability of the Developed FurniCraft System
P086 [Normal] The researchers evaluated the developed system using the System Usability Scale (SUS), the ten-item instrument identified in Chapter III. A total of 52 respondents completed the questionnaire using a five-point Likert scale. Following the standard scoring method, responses to odd-numbered items were adjusted by subtracting one from the selected rating, while responses to even-numbered items were adjusted by subtracting the selected rating from five. Each adjusted item contributes zero to four points; the sum is scaled by 2.5 to produce a SUS score from 0 to 100.
P087 [Normal] The available survey results are aggregated counts for each response option by question. Therefore, the mean SUS score can be calculated from the tallies, but the distribution of individual respondent scores, its standard deviation, and subgroup comparisons cannot be computed without the original respondent-level forms.
P088 [Normal] Table 4.2. Distribution of SUS Responses and Adjusted Totals (n = 52)
P089 [Normal] Each question in Table 4.2 contains 52 responses. Positive-worded odd-numbered questions generally received ratings of four or five, while negative-worded even-numbered questions generally received ratings of one or two. This response pattern is consistent with favorable perceived usability. The adjusted totals of all ten questions sum to 1,777.
P090 [Normal] Table 4.3. Overall System Usability Scale (SUS) Result
P091 [Normal] The overall SUS score was computed using the following equation:
P092 [Normal] Mean SUS = (Total adjusted item contributions / Number of respondents) × 2.5
P093 [Normal] Mean SUS = (1,777 / 52) × 2.5 = 85.4327 ≈ 85.43
P094 [Normal] Based on the interpretation scheme provided in Table 3.6 of Chapter III, scores above 80.3 correspond to Grade A or Excellent. The computed mean score of 85.43 therefore places FurniCraft in the Excellent usability category. The result indicates that the 52 respondents generally reported a favorable experience when interacting with the system. It supports the third research objective, which is to assess the usability of the developed FurniCraft application.
P095 [Normal] Although the outcome indicates high perceived usability, SUS is a subjective usability measure rather than a direct test of recommendation precision, visual recognition accuracy, reservation completion rates, security, or financial performance. The finding should therefore be interpreted as evidence of perceived ease of use and interaction quality within the surveyed group. As the study employed convenience sampling, the result should not automatically be generalized to all possible furniture buyers.
P096 [Section] Summary of Chapter IV Findings
P097 [Normal] The findings were presented according to the study objectives. First, the existing furniture-selection process involved customer inquiries and comparisons of design, price, materials, finishes, and room suitability, with documented challenges concerning customized orders and some installation-related concerns. Second, FurniCraft was designed and described as an integrated web-based platform that supports product browsing, preference-based recommendation, 2D room visualization, chatbot assistance, reservations, and administrative management. Third, the 52-respondent SUS evaluation yielded a mean score of 85.43, which is classified as Excellent under the interpretation framework established in Chapter III. Together, these results show that the system addresses the identified information and selection needs at the design and functionality level and was perceived favorably for usability by the participating respondents.

P100 [Normal] SUMMARY, CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
P101 [Section] Summary
P102 [Normal] This study, entitled “FurniCraft: An AI-Based Furniture Recommendation System for Lance Furniture Shop,” aimed to design and develop a web-based application that assists customers in selecting furniture according to their budget, preferred style, wood type, varnish finish, and room requirements. The research was undertaken in response to the challenges identified in the shop’s conventional furniture-selection process, including difficulty comparing product specifications, uncertainty about product suitability, and concerns involving cancelled or unclaimed customized orders.
P103 [Normal] Specifically, the study sought to (1) identify the existing furniture recommendation process of Lance Furniture Shop, (2) describe the features of the developed system, and (3) test the usability of the system. The study employed a descriptive-developmental research design and used Rapid Application Development (RAD), covering Requirements Planning, User Design, Construction, and Cutover. Semi-structured interviews and survey questionnaires were identified as primary data-gathering instruments, and convenience sampling was used in selecting participants.
P104 [Normal] FurniCraft was developed as a web-based platform for customers and the Administrator/Owner. The system incorporates furniture catalog browsing, preference and budget filtering, recommendation functions based on content-based and collaborative filtering, 2D room visualization, Gemini-powered chatbot assistance, furniture reservation requests, and administrative management of records and reports. These functions were intended to make furniture information more accessible and support customers in comparing available products before choosing an item or placing a reservation.
P105 [Normal] The researchers assessed usability through the System Usability Scale (SUS). Based on the aggregated questionnaire tallies from 52 respondents, the ten SUS items produced 1,777 adjusted contribution points. This resulted in an overall mean SUS score of 85.43 out of 100, equivalent to Grade A or Excellent according to the evaluation scheme adopted in Chapter III. The evaluation provides evidence that participating users perceived the system to have a high level of usability.
P106 [Section] Conclusions
P107 [Normal] Based on the findings presented in Chapter IV, the researchers drew the following conclusions in accordance with the study objectives.
P108 [Normal] First, the existing furniture-selection and recommendation process of Lance Furniture Shop required customers to consider and compare several product characteristics, such as dimensions, design, budget, wood type, and varnish finish. The documented concerns relating to product suitability and customized orders showed the need for a more organized and informative furniture-selection process. These findings justified the development of a digital recommendation and product-information platform.
P109 [Normal] Second, the developed FurniCraft system provided a design and feature framework for addressing the identified furniture-selection needs. Its catalog, preference filtering, recommendation functions, 2D room visualization, chatbot assistance, reservation feature, and administrator functions were aligned with the stated objectives and scope. The system is intended to assist customers in evaluating furniture options and to help the establishment organize related information. However, the study did not quantify improvements in sales, cancellation rates, or operational costs.
P110 [Normal] Third, the system obtained a mean SUS score of 85.43 from 52 respondents, which corresponds to Excellent usability under the scoring interpretation presented in Chapter III. This indicates that the participants generally perceived FurniCraft to be easy to use and satisfactory as an interactive web-based furniture-selection application. Thus, the third objective of assessing the system’s usability was accomplished for the participating sample.
P111 [Normal] Overall, the study demonstrates the feasibility of applying web-based recommendation functions and AI-supported assistance to a local furniture retail setting. Its clearest measured outcome is strong perceived usability; claims regarding recommendation accuracy, business profitability, and long-term operational impact would require additional evaluation beyond the SUS questionnaire.
P112 [Section] Recommendations
P113 [Normal] In light of the findings and the scope and limitations of the study, the researchers recommend the following improvements and future activities.
P114 [Normal] First, Lance Furniture Shop should regularly review and update product descriptions, dimensions, available materials, varnish finishes, prices, and product images. Accurate and current furniture information is essential to producing relevant recommendations and allowing customers to evaluate available items.
P115 [Normal] Second, future research should evaluate recommendation quality using task-based testing and appropriate performance measures, such as whether suggested items satisfy customers’ stated budget and dimension constraints. Such evaluation would complement the SUS result and establish how effectively the recommendation component identifies suitable products.
P116 [Normal] Third, the 2D room visualization and room-image analysis features should be tested with diverse room layouts and image conditions. Future development may consider improved dimension validation, more reliable visual feedback, and optional three-dimensional visualization, provided these are technically and financially feasible for the business.
P117 [Normal] Fourth, the establishment should provide clear reservation policies and customer-facing guidance, especially for custom-made items. Future development may examine reservation reminders, order confirmation workflows, or other mechanisms that can help manage cancellations and unclaimed orders. Any effect on cancellations should be monitored using actual before-and-after business records.
P118 [Normal] Fifth, the researchers recommend periodic usability assessments involving a wider range of users, including individuals with different levels of digital experience. Collecting respondent-level SUS data, observing actual task completion, and recording user suggestions would provide a more detailed basis for prioritizing interface improvements. Future sampling may also be broadened beyond convenience sampling to improve the generalizability of findings.
P119 [Normal] Lastly, future developers may consider features excluded from the present scope, such as inventory management, delivery tracking, online payments, or a dedicated mobile application, only after evaluating operational needs, privacy and security requirements, available resources, and the business’s readiness for adoption. Any additional feature should be introduced without compromising the simplicity and usability reflected in the current evaluation.
